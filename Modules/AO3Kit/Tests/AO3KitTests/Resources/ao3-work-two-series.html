  1. CAN/SWE: Round Robin

Augustin has never wanted to win something so ultimately inconsequential in his entire goddamn life. 

It’s a circus show, but it’s better than the All Star Game by every metric that exists—any game where Augustin wears the red maple leaf on his chest is the best game that he’ll ever play, fewer and farther between the older he gets. Next year will be his second and probably his last Olympics, but this isn’t a trial run for that like everyone is saying. This is another gold medal to bite, another win to add to his pristine international record.

“How do you feel about this tournament?” someone in the media scrum asks, and Augustin resists the urge to roll his eyes again. He can’t be caught rolling his eyes with an A on his chest and he definitely can’t repeat what he said about sticking it to the United States at dinner last night. 

“Any opportunity to prove why this is our sport is a good opportunity for me,” he decides to say. In the back of the scrum, Chris—who was at dinner last night and heard exactly where Augustin wanted to stick it—is laughing. Augustin isn’t sure why he’s hiding back there when half of the media scrum is gathered around his fiance across the room, but it’s none of his business. 

“What about Kent Parson?” someone else asks, “You’re fresh off of a second Cup together, and you’re going for a third this season. How does it feel to be playing against him?”

“Feels great,” Augustin replies, scratching his nose to hide the edge of his smirk, “You know, I love the Aces, but I’ve been stewing over five years worth of his sh- crap for way too long. It’s payback time.”

“Getting your licks in, eh?” Chris asks in French, and laughs at his own joke while Augustin glares at him.

“Getting a win, for Quebec and for Canada,” he replies. “Besides, you guys might not know this, but Kent looks fantastic in silver.”

The media scrum laughs, and he knows that line will be in every article written between this game and the game against America in a few days. Augustin’s going to catch hell from his agent and Hockey Canada for that one, but he still smiles. This is his turf, his homeland, and his fucking game. 

It’s not a lie. He’s been waiting for this one.

“Don’t have any questions for anyone else?” He asks Chris as the media scrum shuffles towards where Chris’s fiance is sitting with his head down and dark hair hanging over his eyes as they stick microphones in his face. This tournament is not the first time Augustin’s met Jordan Dunleavy, but it is the first time that he’s played with him, and he won’t lie: when Jordan is good, he’s one of the best goalies Augustin has ever seen by a country mile, but he’s not always good. 

Chris shrugs as he wanders away, “What’s the point? I can always ask him when he’s more relaxed.”

“We’ll add that to the list of things I didn’t need to know,” Augustin mutters as he leans down to tie his skate laces. Across the room, Zimmermann is doing the same, but Augustin can see his broad shoulders shaking with uncharacteristic laughter, the jersey hanging behind him carrying the same white A over its heart.

Team Canada’s captain this year is the oldest guy in the room—Matt Eaton, the pride of Prince Edward Island and Augustin’s old captain from his singular year with the Habs. He’d been shocked to be given a letter he’s already worn for the last decade. Everyone thought that the captaincy would be a tossup between East and West, Zimmermann or Berenger, an overinflated rivalry that neither of them care about except for all the ways that they do care—winning the fucking game, and that second, other thing that they resolutely don’t talk about.

The phone call where they decided to decline the co-captaincy in favour of Eaton had been very awkward.

Across the room, Zimmermann meets his eyes and shrugs as he straps on his elbow pads. Augustin hasn’t been on a team with him since the 2010 World Juniors, and he can tell that they’re both sincerely hoping that this tournament doesn’t end the same way as that one had. 

Least of all because Kent will be fucking insufferable, and this time it will be Augustin’s problem.

There are no easy teams in this round robin, no up and coming hockey programs that are just happy to be included. Augustin surveilles the other team during warm-up, waves at Allie when he lifts a hand in greeting. A pit begins to deepen in his stomach as he watches them warm up. What the Swedes lack in physical mass, they make up for in speed and dexterity, and they have the best goalie in the league between two pipes. 

“Hey, Lando,” Augustin says when Elias Landvik skates over and stops primly at the red line to greet him. He looks good in the yellow of his homeland, even though Augustin is much more used to—and prefers—seeing him in the black and silver of an Ace. “Ready to lose?”

“No,” Lando says in that strange, detached way of his, his wide dark eyes unblinking. “I have your number, and you’re old.” 

Augustin grins in spite of himself as he slowly skates away towards the bench, “Not gonna mince words, are you?” 

“No,” Lando says again, but Augustin sees him narrow his eyes behind the cage of his helmet when Augustin makes sure that Arya Mousevi, Team Canada’s hotshot young defenceman, is the last one off the ice instead. Lando might know all his best moves on the ice, but there are a few things that Augustin still has over him, and knowledge of his superstitions is one of them.

Aboveground, the stands are deafening, their roars like a crashing waterfall muted by a layer of stone and rubber. Among the crowd is Kent, who will be resolutely refusing to cheer too loudly or wear red and white. Augustin can hardly hold it against him. He thinks wearing a Team USA jersey and applauding their goals would give him full-body hives.

Inside the room, the world is silent. Augustin strips half out of his gear like always, rubs his eyeliner pencil over his waterline and washes his hands twice to get the stickiness of sweat off of them. The only real sounds are murmurs and velcro, and the occasional laugh from the goalies in the corner. Half the room has never played in a best-on-best international tournament before, and the nerves are tangible.

Augustin reaches out and knocks a hand against the kneepad of Callum MacPherson, the team’s youngest player, sitting silently in the stall next to him. He wasn't even meant to be there—he'd been flown up from the Bahamas two nights ago after one of the original forwards fractured his wrist in practice. Augustin can see the pressure pushing him down onto the bench like an empty can beneath a tire. “You okay, Mac?”

“Yeah,” Mac says, determined face unable to draw attention from his green pallor as the coaches file in. “Just…yeah. I’m good.” 

“Okay,” Augustin says, “If you need to throw up, stand at the back of the line when we go out.”

That startles enough of a laugh out of Mac that Augustin’s pretty sure that the joke will stay that way. 

The lineup is read out by Patrice Vaillancourt, the uncontested greatest Hab to ever wear the jersey. Augustin had a poster of him on his wall as a kid, and feels all of five years old again when Vaillancourt points directly at him, and says in his rough joual accent, “Starting on the left, number seven, Augustin Berenger.”

Even Jack beams when he’s announced as the starting centre, knee bouncing like a child as he catches Augustin’s eye across the room. They’ve been practicing together for the last week and a half for exactly this, a blank canvas meticulously molded into the shape of the most anticipated starting lineup in national history. There’s nothing to relearn, no pre-existing foundation to build from—the one time in two decades that they weren't facing each other at centre ice, Jack sat on the bench while Augustin took the first puck drop. 

But that was a thousand years ago. This is now, and it’s only everything.

When he’s done, Vaillancourt folds up the piece of paper and looks at them all for just a moment, as if seeing them for the first time. A small smile bends the corners of his stern mouth, and he wordlessly motions for Eaton to stand, slipping back amid the small crowd of childhood heroes assembled in the corner of the room. 

Eaton stands and clears his throat, the flush of adrenaline highlighted by the red of his jersey. 

“You never think that you’ll wear this jersey until you do,” he says, “Think about what it means to you. Think about what you were willing to do to wear it. Whatever it was, however many years and sleepless nights and wind sprints it took, you have to do it again today. Here. Now.”

The room is silent. Eaton is a man of few words. Every single one that he says takes on a life of its own, standing in the middle of the room with them.

“We’re doing this,” he continues, making sure to look at every single one of them, turning slowly on his heels, “For the kids on the ponds, the kids who shoot pucks at the garage door, and the kids who can’t play. From coast to coast to fucking coast. Every game you play for the next two weeks is the game of your life, and theirs. Let’s show the fucking Swedes whose game this is. Whose game?”

“Our game!” Augustin shouts alongside the rest of them—the old vets nearing retirement, the ones on the wrong side of thirty like he is, the new generation of stars in their prime and the young guns with shiny eyes. Tonight, they are all one entity. One voice. One body.

One game.

The anthem plays, the lights go up. There is red, and there is white, and across the ice, the yellow and blue stands out as unsuitable, incorrect. Out of place. 

The air is so fucking cold in his lungs, and his smile is vicious. 

Our game.

The Swedes give as good as they get. They’re a smaller country, but no less proud and no less hungry, and they breathe ice into their lungs from birth just like any Canadian child. They match the Canadians blow for blow, almost shot for shot. Lando lets in two goals in the first that no human being could have stopped, but Jordie lets in a bad goal in the second period that opens the scoring for Sweden, and it only goes downhill from there.

The third period ends in a tie, three to three, and Augustin’s mouthguard chewed to bits as he stares up at the score displayed on the jumbotron at the Centre Bell.

His province, his game, his muscles aching and lungs burning. He’s old now, or at least he feels that way, all of thirty-two, and now it’s his job to remind the younger players that games aren’t over until they end, and that losses don’t happen until they’re back in the dressing room getting into their suits. 

“Just one goal,” he tells them, Jack and Eaton nodding beside him, “Take shots where you can, play smart. Play like you mean it. If we lose, we lose, and there’s nothing we can do about it except play this one to the end, and win the next one.” 

Across the room, Jordie stares vacantly into space, lips pinched. If Augustin didn’t know any better, he’d be scared shitless by the thousand-yard stare, but he’s been briefed. He waves the coaches away when they try to approach the goalie, and is rewarded by Jordie snapping back to life right on schedule as the rest of the team gets ready to troop back out, pulling his mask over his face with an aura of genial calm.

“You’re better at the speech thing than me,” Jack says offhandedly to him as they wait to troop back out to the bench. His Quebecois accent isn’t pronounced anymore—it never really was, since he went to an Anglo school until Juniors and his parents live in the wealthy English enclave of Montreal. Still, Augustin can hear the echoes underneath, and finds an odd comfort in it.

“I got soft,” he replies. Jack smiles wryly as the lights go down.

“Only off the ice.”

He feels every single year of his career as overtime winds down, and if he wasn’t so intent on chasing down the puck and pelting Lando with it, he thinks he could see those past iterations of himself behind him if he craned his neck. There isn’t time to look. There is only the puck, and the net, and the wide expanse of ice. 

“Berenger enters the zone, dumps it into the corner, picked up by Eaton. Eaton dishes it to Mousevi on the blue line, takes a shot- SCORES! Augustin Berenger tips it past Landvik into the back of the net! The hometown hero wins it for Canada with his first goal of the tournament!”

Somewhere between the flashing lights, the tackling hugs, his heart beating in his mouth and the yawning maw of the tunnel to the dressing room, Augustin sees a glimpse of his family celebrating on the jumbotron: his crying mother, his father being shaken by those around him, his sister chugging a beer as Jeff Troy swings his Team Canada jersey over his head beside her. Beside him, Kent is dressed in muted blue, just a small smile on his face before the cameras cut away.

“You had better fucking win this one, Gus. I’m the only one who gets to beat you.”

The press is a congratulatory blur where he spits out some bland response to questions about that final goal, which he paradoxically remembers like seeing it for the first time and can’t describe at all. Inside his veins, his blood sings. He can hardly hear what he's saying, just grins as he meets Jack's eye across the media scrum and gets a shared, hungry smile in reply from between the forest of microphones.

"Is there anything you think you need to improve for next game?"

My celebrations. "Well, you know, the boys really-"

“You fucking asshole!” 

The entire room turns its head to where their goalie is standing straight up, mouth open with utter outrage. Augustin cranes his head over the crowd in front of him, and it resolves into view: Chris Patenaude, bane of hockey locker rooms everywhere, smiling pleasantly as he holds a recorder through the crowd of reporters flocking towards the noise.

“Do you want to answer the question or yell at me some more?” he asks, voice light and undeniably gloating. Augustin’s starting to understand why he was hiding on this side of the room for the whole day.

“I want to yell at you some more,” Jordie retorts, “But to answer your question, yes, I am very aware of the goal I let in during the second period. It’s not going to happen again. And for the record, the yelling and swearing is unrelated to the question he asked, and is actually the result of this…guy lying to me that he wasn’t going to cover this event.”

“Write that down, ESPN,” Chris says to the guy next to him, who is struggling not to laugh. 

“Seriously, Pats, were you, like, hiding from me?” Jordie continues, “Did you- wait, where the hell is- Berenger!”

“I didn’t have anything to do with this,” Augustin calls over the crowd, the journalists all shifting towards the commotion as they clear away from his stall “I thought you knew he was here for the event, considering that we all had dinner together last night.”

“I- I thought that was unrelated,” Jordie splutters, face reddening, “He said he had time off- you guys are setting me up. This is a setup!” 

“No,” Chris says, and Augustin is the only one who can hear the silky glide to his words and know what that means, “It’s motivation.”

“Oh my God,” he mutters as Jordie flushes, striding past Zimmermann towards the showers, “Keep them away from me.” 

“As if you’re not worse than they are,” Zimmermann replies under his breath as he walks by.

“As if you’re not worse than both of us,” Augustin retorts, slamming the brim of Zimmermann’s baseball cap over his eyes. He calls over his shoulder, "Drinks on me, but only two each! We have more games to win after this."

The raucous cheer that follows him into the showers drowns out the sound of Jordie and Arya calling him cheap, and he feels as though his heart is flying.

  1. CAN/USA: Round Robin

This is Kent’s worst nightmare. 

“This is my worst nightmare.”

Across the ice, Augustin and Jack are staring at him, their heads leaned together as they whisper to each other. Kent wants to kill them both, but it’s probably too late. As far as he knows, he’s about to be murdered. 

“I don’t like the way that they’re looking at us,” agrees one of his alternates, Ethan Barker. “I feel like they can see when and how I’ll die. Like, one can see when, and the other can see how-” 

“I get it,” Kent snaps.

“They kinda look related,” Pierce McMurray, his other A, muses. Kent needs to throw up right now, because he’s right. Augustin is like a skinnier, more elegant model of Jack’s broader, superhero looks, but they stand at about the same height in skates, and ever since Augustin finally started carrying weight through the season and cutting his hair shorter—which Kent considers a crime against humanity, by the way—they look similar from the back, too. It’s worse when they’re wearing baseball caps.

Kent’s going to have a heart attack.

“I’m having unholy thoughts,” he says out loud, mostly by accident. Across the ice, Augustin is smiling, a flicker of white teeth against pale skin and red mouth, like he knows exactly what the two of them look like together. He puts his hand low on Jack’s back as their heads lean closer together, and Kent feels his teeth grind together.

“Like murder?” Pierce asks.

“Yeah,” Kent replies faintly when Jack leans down to whisper something in Augustin’s ear that makes the grin more evil than it already is. “Like murder.”

Augustin turns toward his own net, tapping Jack on the ass with a glove as he skates away. Kent’s pretty sure he makes an audible noise at that.

“We need to win this game,” he says. There’s national pride on the line, sure, and also a pretty sizable wager about who’s going to be doing the next year’s worth of dishes, and it’s not like this is the first time that Kent has stood across a rink from Augustin and Jack both wearing the same jersey. But now, this is about Kent’s personal dignity. “We really need to win.”

The Canadian team has the best forwards in the world on their team, some of the most solid defencemen in the league, but goaltending is their weak spot. Kent hates to say it, because he likes Jordan Dunleavy for multiple reasons, but this isn’t the time for soft-hearted sympathies. Jordan already clearly knows what everyone knows. “Shots on net, just pelt him. He’s slow to get up and down, so use that. Don't let him collect the rebounds.”

“I can’t believe they’re starting him in net again,” Pierce says as they put their helmets back on and get ready to go out. He’s one of the younger players, the second-youngest captain of a team in league history. They called him the next Kent when he was drafted, even though Kent was only twenty-six at the time. “Like…he’s a great guy, super nice, but he’s old and they’re setting him up for failure. What the hell do they see in him?”

“Offside and Sportsnet both think that he should start every game,” Barker comments, mouth compressed into a thin line, “So they gotta see something.”

Kent doesn’t say what he thinks Offside in particular sees in Jordan Dunleavy, even though the joke is so easy. “Let’s just get the win. Also, I resent the old comment. He’s only a few months older than me.”

“You’re old, too,” Barker says ruthlessly, his beard thicker than anything Kent could grow.

He would never admit it out loud, but the Canadians are always the favourite to win for a reason. They're trained from birth for this; their lullabies are fuzzy old broadcasts, and they learn to skate before they can walk. By God, they love this game. They built it from scratch, and live in it like an ancestral home. They think that it's theirs.

It makes them arrogant.

The first period starts with three fights right off the whistle and ends in a one-one tie: Jordie's slow to get up and down just like Kent had predicted, but the Canadian defencemen are doing their job and then some. Kent can see them laughing as they pile off the ice for the break between periods, ribbing each other like this is a game of shinny and not the fight of their lives.

"Let's knock them down a few pegs," he urges, smacking helmets as he passes them by in the dressing room. "Let's show the fucking Canadians that their time is over. It's our turn."

When he gets back onto the ice for the second, Jack isn't smiling, but he's doing his best imitation of a gloating grin across the dot. His voice is has the false-sweetness of aspartame as he asks in French, "Hey, Augustin, what do you think happens when we win?"

"Yeah, I wonder," Kent replies before Augustin has the chance, and Jack's face drops just before the puck. He doesn't whiff the faceoff like Kent had hoped, but he does lose it, and it's downhill from there.

Kent knows Augustin better than the pattern of veins and freckles on the back of his hand, knows how to burrow under the unflappable skin and stick there. He doesn't even pretend that he's not going after Team Canada's leading points earner, slashing and chirping all the while. Everyone has noticed, based on the way that the heavier players on Team Canada are starting to go after him for it, but Kent's got fast feet and a mouth on him. Augustin knows that better than anyone.

"God, you're such a fucking shithead," Augustin snaps after an offside whistle, looping back around to where Kent is standing innocently by the faceoff dot, twirling his stick. "I forgot how fucking annoying you are."

"Baby, you're going to hurt my feelings."

"I'm gonna hurt way more than your fucking feelings," Augustin snarls as the other players circle each other, the storm of a post-whistle scrum brewing dark and heavy. Kent leans in close enough to make his breath fog against the glass of Augustin's visor and winks. Augustin shoves him hard enough to knock him back a step, aiming a little too high to be playful.

"Don't fucking touch him," Barlow, one of the American D-men, growls, shoving a hand into Augustin's sternum.

"Oh, what the fuck are you gonna do, Barlow?" Augustin sneers, shoving right back, "Fucking nothing, that's what, with your fucking thick-ass skull. I'm surprised you can even read the name on the back of my jersey, you dumb fuck."

Kent just laughs as Barlow and Augustin wrestle for a few seconds before the refs tug them apart, shoving them towards their positions. A chorus of whistles shrieks as a few feet away, Arya Mousevi attempts to put Pierce in a headlock.

"Watch your fucking back, Parson," Augustin mutters as he skates by, "I'm not playing nice anymore."

Kent grins as he lines up for the faceoff, "We never play nice."

He gets his chance to put his money where his mouth is soon enough. Halfway through the second, Barker fumbles the puck at the blue line, and Augustin capitalizes, sweeping up the puck and breaking down the ice with it. It'd be a sure goal if Kent wasn't on his heels like a dog, nipping and biting until he manages to get his stick up into Augustin's gloves, and gives them a good, hard chop. The puck skips away, skittering directly into the goalie's glove.

He laughs at the sound of the whistle without a penalty, and the rest of his breath is lost as a cannonball slams him into the boards from behind, a hand shoving his head hard into the glass.

"You dirty fucking cheating asshole-"

Kent blows Augustin a kiss as both linesmen tow him, hissing and spitting, towards the penalty box. He's still laughing to himself as he gets up, Augustin's enraged hollers lost in the matching outrage of the crowd as he smacks his stick against the glass and points threateningly at Kent from across the ice, hair flying everywhere as he slams his helmet onto the bench. God, he looks good like that: all dressed up in red, helpless and right where Kent's put him.

"Aren't I good?" He says smugly to Jack as he skates towards the bench, and Jack's stony face doesn't twitch.

Pierce manages to score during the powerplay to put them up by one, and when Augustin comes out of the box, he doesn't even look at Kent as he skates stiffly over to the bench. Kent watches him go, knowing he can feel the gaze on the back of his neck like a breath.

"See you soon, sweetheart," he calls acoss the ice after the buzzer goes. Augustin pauses momentarily in the mouth of the tunnel, Arya Mousevi crashing into his back, before forging onward with noticeably tenser shoulders.

Five years ago, the gloves probably would've come off. Nowadays, Augustin's got a better grip on his temper, so Kent doesn't get anything much more satisfying out of him in the remaining time, other than an empty-netter that sends them sailing past the Canadians in their own damn house. He doesn't gloat out loud once the game ends—there's chirping, and then there's heading directly to divorce court. He simply contents himself with patting their goalie on the head as they watch the last scrum of the game clear up, and admires the view; Augustin with his helmet knocked off, hair wild around his pale face as he shouts at the referee and Barlow in equal measure, a split on his bottom lip bleeding over his chin.

The press scrum is a hostile nightmare thanks to the dearth of Canadian media, and Kent has to stare a silent Chris Patenaude dead in the eye as he's asked by someone else, "Did you think a penalty shot should've been called on you in the second when you slashed Berenger?"

"Doesn't matter what I think," Kent replies. Adrenaline thuds electrically through his limbs, and he can still taste blood in his mouth. "Matters what the ref thinks."

Chris smiles at that, but it's wolfish as he pipes in, "You got into a bit of a dust-up with your Aces teammate, Augustin Berenger, in the middle of the second. Given the high stakes of the tournament, do you think it'll affect how you play when the regular season resumes?"

"Ah, Augustin loves me," Kent replies, his own grin still gloating, "He'll get over it."

He doesn't like the way Chris's eyebrows lift disbelievingly as the scrum moves on, but he supposes that Chris will be too preoccupied licking his boyfriend's wounds to cause Kent any real problems.

They skip the bars that night, since the entire city of Montreal wants them dead. Pierce leads the bribery and raid of the hotel bar's stockroom, and the whole team sprawls out across Kent's king-sized bed and the surrounding carpet, twenty-odd drunk men chanting the old college and USNDP chants that are engraved in their bones as the late night becomes early morning.

"Boston, here we come!" Pierce hollers out the balcony door, met with a few faint Quebecois curses from the street below that Kent refuses to translate even in his head, "How does it feel to lose, fuckers? Yeah, you, down there, I'm talking to you! Kiss my ass! U-S-A! U-S-A!"

"Get him off the balcony before someone throws a bottle at him," Kent tells someone, though he's not even sure who it is through the haze of beer and tequila. "Or he shows his bare ass. I have to head out."

"Where?" their backup goalie, Foggy, wonders out loud as Kent peels himself off the floor, groaning from a sore back, "We're in our hotel. We're in your room."

"He's gonna go gloat," Pierce says, stumbling off the balcony with his beer bottle still safely in hand, "Tell Berenger I said to kiss my fucking ass."

"Sure. He'll definitely take that well," Kent replies as he pulls his coat off of the hanger. "You had all better be out of here when I get back. Go to Ethan's room."

"If you get back," Pierce mutters under his breath.

“I think they’re going to kill you,” Barker says merrily, clearly drunk as a skunk, “We just beat them at home, and the French Canadian fucks aren’t gonna like that, which is why we are DRINKING IN OUR HOTEL ROOMS!”

A cheer goes up, and Kent snorts, the alcohol making him brave and stupid. “I’ll be fine, guys. Gus will keep me safe.” 

“Berenger?” Barker laughs, “Dude, I’d put money on him getting the first go at you. He was pissed as fuck that you stopped him from scoring during the second and added a penalty on top, I could hear him yelling at you from the bench.”

"Naw," Kent says as he checks his pockets for his key card and wrenches open the door, "He loves me. I'll be back."

He could walk to the hotel the Canadians are staying in, but he's easily recognizable and the streets aren't empty. Jordie knows what they’ve been saying about him. 

He’s not as young as he used to be, and definitely not as good as he once was. He is a fantastic starting goalie, top ten in the league by save percentage, but when the opposing teams all have starters in the top three, and the next three are their backups, it’s hard not to notice both his age and skill. He’s got the best defencemen in the world protecting him, and the best forwards in the league going up against him. Kent Parson is a force to be reckoned with when he is an Ace, but when he wears the crest of Team USA, he’s hockey on two legs. Across from him, Pats muffles an ugly snort of laughter in the crook of his elbow.

They have three days between the round robin and the game of their lives, the ultimate redo. Before the tournament began, he'd been resolved to treat this as a practice round for the Olympics. And he's fucking terrified of it.

Jordie is mobbed by them, but when he cranes his neck over their heads, he doesn't see the only member of their corps that he has ever cared about impressing.

"Where is he?" he asks like a madman when Chris's colleague who covers Ottawa is the one who inevitably finds him, just off the ice, and sticks her recorder in his face.

"Working," Blair says kindly, although Jordie despises her for a moment. He retaliates by giving the blandest possible answers which make her nose wrinkle before setting off on his search again. He takes a moment in the still-empty dressing room to shed the sweltering top half of his gear, leaving his lower pads on and medal hanging against his black dry-fit shirt resumes his search.

It feels as though he's looked everywhere, medal heavy against the centre of his chest, until he comes upon a dark hallway just off the main tunnel. The scrum has migrated into the dressing room, and Jordie can hear the pop of champagne bottles echoing through the halls along with the whoops as he passes by befuddled and vaguely congratulatory arena staff and ice crew workers, looking in all the dark nooks and crannies

"Jordie."

He turns to see Pats standing in the opening of the small, empty utility hallway, chest heaving like he's run a mile, press pass still swaying against his chest.

"Look," Jordie says, thumbing the ribbon of his medal. He feels high. "We're matching."

"Christ," Pats swears, striding towards him with his cane thudding over the floor, "I got caught up interviewing Eaton, and then Zimmermann- I've been looking for your ridiculously tall ass everywhere."

"Well, I'm here," Jordie grins, "Pull out your microphone."

Pats doesn't pull out a microphone. He just launches himself into Jordie's sweaty torso, arms wrapping around his neck. Jordie clings like a lifeline, burying his nose into skin that smells of aftershave and ice, the tips of Pats's toes barely brushing the floor as he's half-lifted into the air. His cane clatters the ground with a loud thud.

"You were perfect," Pats mutters, his hand buried in Jordie's disgusting, sweat-damp hair. "God, Jordie, you were- that was- putain, better than I could have dreamed."

Something wet touches Jordie's neck, and when he tries to pull back, Pats won't let go. "Are you crying?"

A sniffle, and then mutinously, "No."

Jordie turns his head as best as he can under the grip, struggling to pepper kisses all over his fiance's face in their current positions, and that's what makes Pats surrender. Neither of them even look around to see if anyone is watching before Pats's mouth, warm and hungry, is on his.

"Parfait," Pats murmurs every time that they part to breathe, his nose pressing cold against Jordie's flushed face and stubble scraping his skin. His hands come up to cup Jordie's face, "Tellement parfait."

"Jords."

It's Zimmermann who interrupts them, still in all his gear and staring at them with humour in his blue eyes as he leans against the wall. Jordie clears his throat politely as he places Pats back on his feet. Pats, with no such reservations, just says baldly, "Some of us have to work, you know. Nous n' pouvons pas tous nous embrasser au milieu d'la patinoire."

"You're thirty-six years old, Christophe, you can do whatever you want." Zimmermann chides, beckoning Jordie with a hand, "People want to pour champagne on you."

"Awesome," Jordie says as they make their way to the dressing room, and then he's being covered in champagne, and Chris is filming him from the doorway even though journalists aren't allowed in the room. Jordie opens his mouth wide and sticks out his tongue like he's catching snowflakes, eyes closed against the sweet spray as it trickles through his hair and under his neckline.

They go to a club after, because there's not enough alcohol in the world to match their euphoria but they're certainly gonna fucking try. Pats and Mac, who plays for Boston, choose it; the kind of long, loud, low room that is both too dark and too bright, somewhere that Jordie would have never thought Pats would step a single foot in.

"I spent four years trying to get in here in college," Pats shouts over the music. His white dress shirt is unbuttoned to his navel, and Angelique Berenger is draped over his shoulders, "It only took me an entire national team to get me in!"

Jordie, an untold number of shots deep, just throws his head back and laughs. In the shadows and thudding music that matches his heartbeat, he doesn't care who sees him reel Pats in by the lapels and kiss him soundly on the mouth—the throbbing strobe lights disguise them enough anyways. Pats's hands roam under Jordie's completely unbuttoned shirt, rubbing the countless bruises on his torso tenderly with his thumbs before he pulls away with a cut-off gasp and says, teeth flashing white, "More shots."

"More shots," Jordie agrees, and admires the way the lights flicker off of the silver in his hair.

Sometime after one in the morning, Augustin slips out unnoticed by anyone else. Jordie, sitting on a couch with some of the WAGs because his legs are starting to ache fiercely, watches him slip his ticket and a bill to the coat check, shrugging on the dark wool. He's sober, like always, in all the ways that Jordie is not.

Someone wins, so someone has to lose. Jordie has no doubt that if it were the other way around, Kent would already be knocking on Augustin's hotel room door.

"He'll be fine," someone says beside him, and Jordie turns to see Angelique Berenger crash half onto his lap, slinging her legs over his and wrapping an arm around his shoulders to pull his head closer to her lips, "He's just going to go lick someone else's wounds."

"Gross," Jordie says, looking across the room to see where Pats and Jeff Troy are standing at the bar staring at them with dark eyes and parted lips. They must make a sight together; both of them tall, Angelique with her skirt riding up her athletic legs and Jordie with his shirt open, a hand settled chastely on her knee. He grins and leans over to say, "People are going to get the wrong idea if we keep sitting like this. Never seen Pats look like that before."

"I know, that's the fun part," Angelique says, tossing her hair over her shoulder as she stands and holds out a hand, "Come and dance, you fucking giant. You've earned it."

They last about two songs together before a hand wraps around Jordie's wrist and pulls him off the dance floor. It doesn't stop until they're at the coat check. Pats slides two tickets across the counter and turns to Jordie, "Button your shirt."

"Yes, sir," Jordie says jokingly, but a shiver runs up his spine at the authoritative tone as he dutifully buttons his shirt closed and accepts his coat from the moony-eyed attendant. He shrugs it on as they make their way into the cold air. "I was still celebrating."

"We danced," Pats says crisply, sticking out his free hand to hail a cab. "We drank, and now I'm going to take you back to the hotel and-"

"No," Jordie grins, his voice dropping low, "Don't tell me, I want to dwell on the possibilities."

They manage to keep to themselves in the taxi, the lobby, the elevator—Pats has always been absurdly disciplined, which is why he still has the body of a professional athlete almost two decades out of his career. He even manages to shut and bolt the door with the 'do-not-disturb' sign hanging outside before the buttons of Jordie's shirt are scattered over the floor.

"If I knew this is what it would take," Jordie gasps from under the onslaught as his back hits the bed, "I would've invited you to the IIHF World Championships that one time."

"Mmh," Pats says, from somewhere around his hips, "God, you still taste like champagne."

Thumbs hook into his waistband and pull down without further fuss. With most of the exhaustion in his muscles catching up to him all at once, Jordie is deeply content to let Pats nudge his knees apart and get to work.

He falls asleep just before dawn and wakes up still drunk at around eleven in the morning. The first thing he sees is Pats sitting at the edge of the bed wearing only boxers and his knee brace, cradling Jordie’s medal in his hands. The sunlight reflects off the gold, a circle of light on the ceiling that is half-obscured by the shadow of Pats’s face and hair like a waning moon. 

 Jordie sits up as quietly as he can, the air still and silent. He leaves Pats to grieve hockey whenever the urge hits him—less and less as the years go by, but always with the acute kind of grief that stabs between ribs and into the heart. 

Eventually, Pats stands on his own, limping over to the medal case and carefully depositing the gold disc into the soft velvet cushion. He says without turning, "I ordered breakfast, it'll be here in a few minutes."

"Good," Jordie sighs, letting his head hit the pillow with a thud. Especially compared to the smooth, muscled planes of Pats's back, he's positively spotted with bruises. He can't imagine moving anytime soon. "I want to stay in this bed all day. I've earned it."

Pats sits back on the edge of the mattress with a sigh, twisting to look at him ruefully, "We made a mess of this bed, we should-"

"Increase the mess, I agree," Jordie says sagely, hooking his foot around Pats's elbow and dragging him up towards him. Pats follows willingly, uncharacteristically pliable as Jordie kisses him. In the room next door, he can hear Mac and Mouse groaning about their hangovers through the thin wall, and can only be glad that they came back just before sunrise.

“Next year,” Pats whispers against his mouth, hot and sweet, “The one you bring me will have Olympic rings on it.”

“Should I put it in my vows?” Jordie grins as teeth scrape the skin of his jaw and the space beneath his ear, “I swear to love you in sickness and in health, for better or worse, and also I’ll bring you home an Olympic gold, ‘til death do us part, et. cetera.”

Pats pulls back, mouth pulled into a lazy grin, “And a Stanley Cup if you don’t mind.”

“Quebec’s very own hoping for a Leafs Cup,” Jordie murmurs, closing the distance between them, “Hell has frozen over.”
