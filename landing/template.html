Nectar

Read fic the way you read a book. Keep your place, keep your library.

Nectar is an iOS app that opens any AO3 work, saves your reading position and caches what you read, so a long fic is always waiting where you left it. It shows each work with its own author's styling, and adds notes, highlights, stats and themes on top.

What it does

Everything here is in the app today.

I

A timeline that knows fic

Word count, chapters, completion, fandom, relationships, characters, ratings, warnings and series show on every card, with a progress bar drawn from how far you have scrolled.

II

Reads like AO3

Each work loads with the creator's own styling on, then adds saved position, offline-friendly caching and a distraction-free reader.

III

One book, many copies

The same work in two collections, or re-imported from Calibre, is recognised as one book. Mark it read, starred or loved once and it applies everywhere.

IV

Live chapters

Fetch new chapters of an in-progress work on demand, with rate-limit backoff and a guard that will not overwrite good text with a short or bad fetch.

V

Notes and highlights

Save quotes and write notes as you read. Fix typos and grammar with text replacement, or swap Y/N for a name.

VI

Stats and Screen Time

Track your reading habits, or set yourself a limit. Both are kept on your phone.

VII

Bring your own library

Filter works on AO3 in your browser, paste the search link as a feed, or load a list of links to import your open tabs. Nectar also works with Ambrosia, a local fanfiction library, including large collections over a dedicated sync route.

VIII

Back up everything

Export your library, settings and reading history to a .zip, then restore or merge it later on this device or another.

Fair dealing

What Nectar promises, and what it will not do.

  • Free and open source. Always. Read the code, or build the app yourself.
  • Does not distribute fics. Works are fetched from AO3 only when you open one.
  • Respects AO3. It does its best to honour AO3's terms of service, servers and policies.
  • Private by design. Sign-in happens in Safari and your login details stay on your phone. Your reading history never leaves it.
  • No account needed. You can read without ever logging in.

Good to know

  • Beta software. Expect rough edges while it is under active development.
  • Made for AO3 and Ambrosia. It should work with any RSS or JSON feed, but it is not a general feed reader.
  • It keeps what you open. The cache is archival and never cleans itself up, so subscribing to a busy news feed will fill your storage.
  • Sideloaded. It is not on the App Store, so installing takes a few extra steps.

It stopped opening after a week
With a free Apple ID, a sideloaded app's signature expires after about 7 days. If you installed with Sideloadly, reinstall the same IPA and it opens again, with no rebuild needed. A paid Apple Developer account extends this to a year.
I can't install a fourth sideloaded app
By default you can only install 3 sideloaded apps. LiveContainer lets you install more.
I would rather not use AltStore
Sideloadly also works. Download Nectar-unsigned.ipa from the latest release, drag it into Sideloadly, and sign in with your Apple ID. You can also build it yourself from source; the README has the steps.
Read this first.

Nectar is unofficial and not associated with Archive of Our Own. AO3 recommends using the site in your browser, and warns that a third-party app you sign in to could gain access to your username and password. Nectar does not require you to log in. Please read the AO3 post on why there is no official app before you decide.

Your data

Stays with you

  • Your reading history and progress stay on your device and are never shared.
  • Nectar signs you in through Safari, and your login details stay on your phone.
  • It contacts AO3's servers only when you open a work.
  • The only privacy permission it declares is adding images to your Photos, so you can save images from an article.
